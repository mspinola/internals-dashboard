How to read this

How to read this
  • Nasdaq stocks at a new 52-week high minus those at a new 52-week low, on the day. Below zero is a headwind.

When the headwinds lined up

QQQ since 2008, daily. Marks show the sessions each headwind was on. Select a row's name to switch it off or on.
How to read this
  • One row per headwind, marked on every session it was on, under the price. The bottom row marks the sessions all six were on at once, and the shading behind the price is the same count: light for five, darker for all six.
  • Select a row's name to switch it off or on: the bottom row and the shading then count only the rows that are on, so you can ask when any set of them lined up. "Only all on at once" drops the lighter band.
  • The bottom row is FOMO, the share of Nasdaq stocks above their 5-day average, on 0 to 100. Under 20 (the cool band) is washed out, 80 and over (the warm band) stretched. On a window of a year or so it is a daily bar: the high-to-low line, the open a tick left, the close a tick right. Every window marks the sessions that closed in either band, and on longer windows, where a daily bar is too thin to read, the marks are all it draws. It is never in the count, and starts in late 2018 where its series does.
  • The window buttons zoom every row together. Point at a session (or tap it) for its date and the rows that were on. Your window, shading and rows are remembered on this device.
  • Caruso's reading: strong uptrends show few headwinds and serious corrections show them stacked. Expect a few short false signals; the aim is to stay with the major trend, not to be perfect.
  • The history starts where the youngest series does (JNK, late 2007). It shows when the headwinds lined up and nothing more: no claim is made here about what followed, and none has been tested.

Nasdaq stocks above their 5-day average · FOMO

The share of Nasdaq Composite stocks above their own 5-day average, past year. Not a trend signal.
How to read this
  • The share of Nasdaq stocks above their own 5-day average: short-term impulse buying and selling. A tactical check at the extremes, not a trend measure, and never in the count.
  • Above 80 is stretched: almost everyone has been buying, which Caruso treats as a likely short-term trap.
  • Below 20 is washed out: almost everyone has been selling, short-term fear. A reading climbing back out of it is recovering.
  • Caruso uses it as context for net new lows: with fear, usually a pause; without fear, a real breakdown. Between the named zones it offers no edge either way.

Market anomalies

Days a usual relationship between two markets did not hold. To be aware of: not counted as a headwind.
How to read this
  • Each card compares two markets' closes, session by session, over the last 20 sessions. A triangle is that market's close, up or down on the day; a shaded column is an anomaly day.
  • Red is the pressure side, green the relief side. Point at a column (or tap it) for its date and both changes.
  • The three pairs and their colours are the Caruso Insights dashboard's. They are context only: nothing here has been tested as a signal.

VIX vs. SPY

Normal
How to read this
  • The VIX and the S&P 500 usually move as mirror images: the VIX rises when the market sells off and falls when it rallies.
  • An anomaly is a day they close the same way. Both up is red: the VIX climbing with the market, the pressure Caruso associates with tops. Both down is green: the VIX easing even as the market falls.
  • Caruso notes the VIX was climbing alongside the S&P 500 before the 2019, 2020 and 2024 tops, and treats it as a yellow flag, not a reason to go to cash.

JNK vs. TLT

Anomaly
How to read this
  • JNK (high-yield bonds) usually moves with Treasuries like TLT. Both are dividend-adjusted here, since a payout would otherwise read as a down day.
  • An anomaly is a day they close opposite ways. TLT up with JNK down is red: money leaving credit for safety. TLT down with JNK up is green.
  • Caruso's example: in the April 2025 selloff TLT fell when it would normally have risen.
